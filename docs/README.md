## Overview of OctoAcme project management processes
OctoAcme’s process begins with initiation, where teams confirm the business need, define stakeholders, and document a concise project one-pager with goals, success metrics, timeline, dependencies, and initial risks. Once the initiative is approved, the team moves into planning to break the work into a prioritized backlog, estimate scope, identify dependencies, and define the definition of done. This creates a shared foundation for delivery and helps ensure that the team is working toward clear, measurable outcomes.

During execution, delivery is coordinated through a consistent rhythm of standups, project board tracking, PR review standards, and escalation paths for blockers. Product managers prioritize value, project managers coordinate schedules and communication, and developers, QA, and stakeholders collaborate on implementation and validation. Quality is treated as part of the delivery flow through CI checks, testing requirements, security scans, and acceptance criteria that must be met before work is considered complete.

Communication is a core part of the workflow. OctoAcme emphasizes regular updates, clear ownership, and a single source of truth for status information. Weekly syncs, milestone reviews, stakeholder briefings, and structured templates for status and incident communication keep teams aligned and help surface risks early. The process also includes explicit escalation paths so issues can move from team-level triage to sponsor-level attention when they have business impact.

At release time, teams validate readiness with smoke tests, rollback planning, and post-deploy verification before shipping to production. After delivery, the team reviews what went well, what could improve, and what actions should be captured as backlog items or follow-up work. This retrospective loop reinforces continuous improvement and helps the organization turn lessons learned into better execution over time.

## How to use this repository
Use the overview and role documents to understand the general operating model, then consult the specific process guides for initiation, planning, execution, release, and improvement activities. These documents are designed to be practical, lightweight, and easy to adapt to real projects while maintaining consistency across teams.
